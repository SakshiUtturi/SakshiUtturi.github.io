Uber: Find My Ride

The driver has arrived. Why can't rider and driver find each other?Market research, user interviews, product ideas
Market
India
Research
10 in-person interviews: nine riders, one driver partner
Delivered
Research document, deck, recorded walkthrough

Find My Ride is about the last 50 metres of a pickup. The app says the car is here, but the rider is at one gate and the driver at another. I looked at where this happens in India, what people do about it today, and what Uber could build.

What I did

  • Sized the opportunity from public data: Uber has about 39 million monthly active users in India, and Indian airports handled about 420 million passengers in FY2025-26.
  • Mapped the alternatives riders rely on today, from phone calls and number plates to Uber's own Spotlight and pickup messages, and where each one falls short.
  • Checked the rules that shape the solution: the 2025 aggregator guidelines, data protection law, and airport pickup zones.
  • Interviewed ten people about their most recent difficult pickup, across airports, stations, tech parks, malls and night travel.

What I heard

  • All ten fall back on a phone call, and the call is itself a pain point: noise, language gaps, a distracted driver.
  • Rider and driver can be at the "same" pin and still be apart, because the pin doesn't say which gate or side.
  • The number plate is the main way to verify a vehicle, but it's hard to read at night and on autos and bikes.
  • Riders accept sharing precise location if it is limited to the pickup window and explained.
"We were both technically at the same pickup location but on different sides."Frequent railway traveller
"A vague pin is the biggest issue."Driver partner

What I proposed

Treat pickup as a two-sided problem, give both sides one shared reference, and solve it in software before touching hardware.

Guided Pickup

The app names one exact gate or landmark and shows it to both rider and driver, with walking directions and a photo of the spot.

Measured by: time from driver arrival to trip start; share of pickups without a call
Vehicle Match

When the car is near, both phones show the same colour and short code, and the rider sees the last four digits of the plate in large type.

Measured by: wrong-vehicle reports; rider confidence, especially at night
No-Call Coordination

One-tap messages such as "I am at Gate 3", translated into the driver's language and read aloud, with location shared only until the trip starts.

Measured by: calls per trip before pickup; cancellations after driver arrival

Limits I'd flag. Ten interviews is a small sample, and the driver's view and the night-safety insight each rest on a single voice. My target of about 8 million riders assumes 20% of Uber's monthly users face a difficult pickup, which is an illustration to be replaced with Uber's trip data.

Zomato: funnel and retention

Where do hungry people give up, and what brings them back?Funnel analysis, cohort analysis, metrics design
Scope
Seven funnel stages, six cohorts, thirteen solutions
Delivered
Strategy document, deck, recorded walkthrough

I broke Zomato's food delivery journey into seven stages, from app open to order delivered, and wrote one testable hypothesis for each. Then I looked at retention across six cohorts and defined how every proposed solution would be measured.

The funnel, and where I'd focus

App open
Restaurant discovery
Menu view
Cart creation
Checkout
Order placed
Order delivered

Bar widths are illustrative. Highlighted stages are the ones I prioritised.

  • Restaurant discovery has the largest reach: every ordering session passes through it.
  • Cart and checkout lose the most intent: these users have already chosen their food, and late fees or failed payments are within Zomato's control.
  • Order delivered links the funnel to retention: a late or wrong order costs every future order from that user.

Example hypotheses

  • If discovery shows a "top 3 for you" shortlist with delivery time and total cost, more sessions will reach a menu, because fewer and clearer options shorten the decision.
  • If the full price, including fees, is visible from the menu page, fewer carts will be abandoned, because there is no price shock at the bill.
  • If new users see a three-order journey with rewards on the second and third order, second-order rate will rise, because early repetition builds the habit.

How I'd measure it

One company North Star: successfully delivered orders per month. Each of the thirteen solutions gets its own ladder of input, leading, output and lagging metrics that feed into it, with guardrails on contribution margin, notification opt-outs and satisfaction so growth isn't bought with discounts.

Limits I'd flag. I had no access to Zomato's analytics, so drop-off points are reasoned from the user journey and I quote no drop-off percentages. The priorities should be confirmed against measured conversion before anything is built.

B2B product strategy

How does "another AI chatbot" become something customers can't easily replace?Segmentation, positioning, three-year strategy
Product
An AI customer support tool for mid-sized businesses
Delivered
Strategy document

The brief: an AI support product is struggling with adoption among mid-sized businesses. Answering questions has become a commodity, with large vendors pricing it at around one to two dollars per resolution. Competing on answers alone is a losing position for a smaller vendor.

Who to serve first

High-volume, digital-first mid-market companies of 200 to 1,000 employees, starting with e-commerce and D2C brands. They have support teams of roughly 15 to 75 agents, already run Zendesk or Freshdesk, and have nobody on staff to build automation.

The problem worth solving

I called it the resolution gap: the distance between answering a question and completing the customer's request. A bot can explain the refund policy, but issuing the refund still means a human moving between the helpdesk, the order system and the payment gateway.

How it grows

Land on one or two request types on top of the existing helpdesk, in draft mode where an agent approves each action. Then expand across request types, channels and teams.

  1. Year 1Resolve

    End-to-end resolution of the top repetitive requests in e-commerce.

  2. Year 2Orchestrate

    Cross-team workflows and insights; fintech and subscriptions next.

  3. Year 3Platform

    Workflow builder and connector library; move toward enterprise.

North Star: share of tickets resolved end to end without a human, at equal or better customer satisfaction.

Limits I'd flag. The diagnosis is a set of hypotheses to validate in customer interviews. The account economics (about $144,000 a year per account) are built on assumptions, not data, and the number of target accounts is not yet counted.

BrightPath Tutoring: building with AI

Can I direct an AI to build a site, and the tooling that checks its own work?AI-assisted front-end build: design system, subagent, custom skill
Tools
Claude and Claude Code
Scope
Front end only: no database, deployment or evaluation
Effort
5 sessions; about 56,700 tokens across the four Claude Code sessions
Delivered
Working site, design system, subagent, skill, evidence document, deck

The assignment was to build the front end of a small-business website with Claude, and to prove the tooling around it works. I chose a tutoring service. The site itself was the easy part; the point was a design system, structured content, an AI reviewer and an automation that each had to pass a test.

A design system where every value traces to a rule

I collected five visual references and had Claude compare them with a first-draft style guide, rule by rule. It found the draft didn't match what I'd collected, so I approved a change of brand colour and typeface. Each of the 15 rules has an ID, each design token names its rule, and each component names its tokens. One value, traced end to end:

  1. On screenThe course title renders as rgb(13, 15, 18)
  2. Component CSScolor: var(--color-ink)
  3. Token--color-ink: #0D0F12, tagged with rule SG-C1
  4. Style-guide ruleSG-C1: headings and primary text use this ink colour
  5. ReferenceThe screenshot the rule was derived from

Content that is validated, not typed into pages

Eight courses live in one JSON file with twelve written rules, and a validator script enforces them before any page is built. A fee typed as "2,500" instead of a number fails the check with the rule it broke.

An AI reviewer that had to pass a test

I set up a read-only compliance subagent that checks code against the design system. To test it, I planted three violations in a real component: a wrong corner radius, off-scale padding and a hard-coded colour.

  • It caught all three, on the right lines, and cited the rule each one broke.
  • Run on the clean component, it passed with zero violations, so it wasn't just flagging everything.

Context that survives a fresh session

I wrote a project context file and then started a brand-new session with only a planning prompt for four more pages. Without being told, the new session named the design rules, the commands and the tooling, and noticed a file that didn't exist yet. That session used 18,729 tokens.

A skill that automates a real chore

Tutors keep the timetable in a spreadsheet, typed inconsistently. I built a reusable skill that converts the export into clean, validated data and rebuilds the schedule page: 15 messy rows in, one duplicate removed, 14 classes out, and a page that went from "coming soon" to a full timetable.

What I took from it as a product person

  • Give every rule an ID. It turns "this looks off" into "this breaks rule SG-S1", for people and for AI.
  • Test the checker before trusting it. A reviewer is only useful once you've seen it catch known problems and pass known-good work.
  • Written context is cheaper than repeated explanation. A good context file meant a new session needed no briefing.

Limits I'd flag. I directed the work and reviewed each step; Claude wrote the code. Three of the six planned pages are built. The subagent follows written instructions, so its results can vary between runs, and a scripted check would make them repeatable.
